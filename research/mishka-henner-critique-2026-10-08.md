# Mishka Henner — No Man's Land (2011–2013)
Research audit: 2026-10-08. Existing artist ID: mishka-henner.

## Central test
Does a work criticizing remote observation actually change how viewers look, or does it also reproduce the gaze it critiques?

**Artist's account.** Henner collected location clues from online forums, searched Google Street View, and selected existing roadside imagery. The project subsequently circulated as print-on-demand books, pigment and billboard prints, a 6:48 video and a 21-minute audio work. The artist treats distance as part of the subject rather than an unfortunate technical defect.

**Independent assessment.** The camera platform genuinely determines perspective, distance, and incidental details; this is stronger than merely decorating conventional pictures with the language of surveillance. But the project cannot establish any individual person's identity or consent from the pictures alone. Recirculation may repeat the unequal relation it criticizes.

## Concept-to-image chain
Online location clues -> remote platform search -> selection of machine-generated views -> editing and publication -> viewers inherit the distant, vehicle-based viewpoint. This chain is demonstrable as a production method, but not as proof of the pictured individuals' identities or experiences.

## Statement-off verdict
Strong: anonymous distance, roadside infrastructure, tiny figures, a repeated car-mounted viewpoint. Weak: the online forum research, the economic interpretation, personal identities and consent. **The work is formally credible as a study of mediated seeing, but socially underdetermined as evidence about particular people.** Its greatest strength and greatest ethical weakness emerge from the same decision to remain remote.
